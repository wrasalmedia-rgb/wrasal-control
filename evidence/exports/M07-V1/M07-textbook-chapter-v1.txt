- At the senior center, Mr. Alvarez has a worsening cough, missed his flu vaccine last year, and shares a bathroom with another older adult on oxygen. A volunteer offers to “save gloves” by reusing them between people.

1. Why Infection Control Matters Across Child, Older Adult, and Disability Settings
Young children in congregate care, older adults with chronic conditions, and people in group living share traits that raise transmission: close contact, shared bathrooms/toys, assistance with toileting/feeding, cognitive changes that affect hygiene, and chronic diseases that worsen outcomes [M07-1][M07-2][M07-3]. Outbreaks (norovirus, influenza, RSV, COVID-19, scabies) and bloodborne exposures (HBV, HCV, HIV) are preventable with consistent, setting-appropriate control — not extraordinary effort [M07-4][M07-5].

Infection control also connects to prior chapters without replacing them:
- Inadequate hygiene, missed medications, or unsafe sharps can look like or contribute to **neglect** (M06 §2) — but infection control steps are about *preventing* harm, not *labeling* harm [M07-2].
- Financial or boundary issues do not replace infection duties — glove reuse “to save money” is both unsafe and improper conduct (M06 §4) [M07-4].
- Reporting a childcare outbreak to health authorities, or a nursing-home infection cluster to DOH, is **not** the same system as child-abuse SCR [M04] or adult APS [M05] — different laws, different numbers (see §9).

Your job: prevent transmission with reliable everyday measures, know when added precautions apply, and notify per agency plan without diagnosing.

2. How Infections Spread — Plain Language
Think in routes, not names [M07-2][M07-3][M07-5]:
- **Contact (direct/indirect):** Touching person, contaminated hands/gloves, toys, doorknobs, bed linens — e.g., norovirus, MRSA, scabies. Hand hygiene + gloves + cleaning interrupt.
- **Droplet:** Large respiratory particles from coughing/sneezing within close distance (often ~3-6 feet) — e.g., influenza. Mask + distance + respiratory hygiene help [M07-5].
- **Airborne:** Small particles that linger and travel — e.g., tuberculosis, measles. Requires ventilation/N95/fit testing per facility and occupational health — not improvised cloth solutions [M07-2][M07-6].
- **Bloodborne:** Blood and certain body fluids — e.g., HBV, HCV, HIV — via sharps, splashes to mucous membranes. No-touch, gloves, no recapping, proper sharps containers [M07-6].
- **Fecal-oral:** Germs from stool to mouth via hands/food/water/surfaces — e.g., norovirus, rotavirus. Hand hygiene with soap/water, diapering/toileting procedures, food safety [M07-4][M07-8].

Age and setting affect risk: infants/toddlers mouth toys and need diapering [M07-8]; older adults have thinner skin and chronic lung/immune conditions [M07-3]; group living adds shared air and bathrooms — but **routes are the same**, precautions adapt to setting, not invented per person [M07-1].

3. Standard Precautions — Every Time, Every Person
Standard Precautions assume **any** person’s blood, body fluids (except sweat), non-intact skin, and mucous membranes may carry infection [M07-4][M07-5]. Apply with everyone, known or unknown status — not only when someone “looks sick.”

Everyday steps [M07-2][M07-4][M07-5]:
- **Hand hygiene:** Soap and water for visibly soiled hands, after toileting/diapering, before food prep/eating, after glove removal. Alcohol-based hand sanitizer (≥60% alcohol) when hands not visibly soiled **except** for stool/vomiting organisms like norovirus/C. difficile — then soap/water required [M07-4]. Do not rely on gloves instead of hand hygiene.
- **PPE based on risk, not routine:** Gloves for touching blood/body fluids/non-intact skin/mucous membranes and for cleaning. Gown if soiling expected. Mask + eye protection if splash/spray likely. **Change gloves between people/tasks, do not wash/reuse** — Jordan’s “save gloves” idea is unsafe [M07-4].
- **Respiratory hygiene / cough etiquette:** Offer tissue and mask where appropriate, distance, encourage covering cough, prompt hand hygiene after [M07-5].
- **Safe handling of linens, waste, food:** Bag linens without shaking; handle waste per agency plan; keep food prep surfaces separate from diapering/toileting [M07-8][M07-10].
- **Injection and point-of-care safety:** Single-use devices — lancets, needles, fingerstick devices — used once per person per time, disposed immediately without recapping or bending [M07-6]; see §6 for overfull container response.

What is not your job: You do not decide diagnosis, isolation type alone, or clearance to return — you follow agency plan + occupational health / infection prevention and document actions taken [M07-6][M07-10].

Senior center example: Mr. Alvarez cough + shared bathroom + oxygen user nearby → Standard Precautions always (hand hygiene before/after contact, mask offer for cough, clean shared surfaces per plan) plus check whether Transmission-Based added precautions are indicated (see §4) — without diagnosing cause [M07-5].

4. Transmission-Based Precautions — Added When Risk Is Higher
When infection-control or public-health assessment indicates, **add** to Standard Precautions — typically in facilities or when agency plan specifies — do not implement alone [M07-2][M07-5][M07-6]:

- **Contact Precautions:** Known/suspected highly transmissible via contact (e.g., active norovirus vomiting/diarrhea, known MRSA wound drainage uncontrolled by dressing, scabies) — gloves + gown for room/entry, dedicated equipment where possible, limit shared activities, reinforce cleaning [M07-4]. For childcare: exclude/ cohort per health guidance; increase toy/surface cleaning [M07-8].
- **Droplet Precautions:** Known/suspected droplet illness (e.g., influenza, RSV) — surgical mask within close distance, private room or spacing where possible, limit movement [M07-5]. Vaccination (see §5) is key prevention alongside.
- **Airborne Precautions:** Known/suspected airborne (e.g., active TB) — negative-pressure room, N95/respirator with fit test, occupational health management — **do not improvise**; follow facility/occupational health immediately and avoid exposure if not fit-tested [M07-6].

After-school example: child with fever/vomiting/diarrhea → isolate per agency plan (separate area, mask not appropriate for vomiting child — focus on contact/fecal-oral precautions: gloves, cleaning, hand hygiene, notify family for pickup, document), following health department exclusion guidance [M07-8][M07-9].

Your role: recognize triggers (“fever plus rash,” “persistent cough with weight loss,” “sudden vomiting cluster”), don Standard Precautions, separate per agency plan, and **notify supervisor / school nurse / infection preventionist / OCC Health promptly** — they decide precaution type and return criteria [M07-6].

5. Vaccination, Documentation, and Exclusion — Support, Not Prescription
Vaccination is among the most effective prevention for many serious infections (e.g., influenza, COVID-19, measles, varicella, pneumococcal, RSV where indicated) especially for children in congregate care and older adults [M07-7][M07-3]. In your role you do not prescribe — you promote, support access, document, and follow exclusion/support per agency/public health guidance [M07-7][M07-9]:

- **Promote:** Share official vaccine information in person’s language, address access (clinic hours, transportation, consent process), reduce misinformation with empathy — document offer and questions referred [M07-7].
- **Document:** Keep vaccination records per setting (school/OCFS childcare immunization requirements [M07-8][M07-9]; nursing home/ACF staff/occupational health records [M07-2]); maintain roster for outbreak response (who is immunized/authorized).
- **Exclusion/support:** When to stay home or move to alternate staffing/activity is **not** personal judgment — follow OCFS/DOH/DOHMH/CDC exclusion guidance and facility policy (e.g., fever thresholds, vomiting/diarrhea 24h, contagious period for pertussis/COVID/influenza, return criteria) [M07-8][M07-9]. Support with food delivery, makeup work, or remote check-ins where allowed — distinguishing exclusion (public-health protection) from **neglect** (failure to meet needs when help available, M06 §2) — do not threaten families with “neglect” for following exclusion guidance [M07-8].

Mr. Alvarez example: missed flu vaccine last year → offer current-season vaccine information, transportation to pharmacy/clinic, document conversation and consent pathway, follow agency plan for seasonal vaccination promotion [M07-7][M07-3] — without pressuring or diagnosing risk alone.

6. Blood, Sharps, and Body Fluids — Safe Every Time
Bloodborne risk is low with precautions, high if shortcuts are taken [M07-6]:

- **Sharps:** Never recap, bend, or remove used needles/lancets. Dispose immediately at point of use into puncture-resistant, labeled sharps container. Replace when **~3/4 full** — do not push to overfill. Adult residence example: overfull sharps container → stop use, secure area, notify supervisor/facilities to replace immediately; do not “make room” by pushing sharps down [M07-6][M07-10].
- **Glucose monitoring:** Fingerstick devices and lancets single-person single-use; clean/ disinfect meter per manufacturer only between persons where approved — otherwise dedicated devices [M07-6].
- **Splashes/spills:** Wear gloves +/- gown/mask/eye protection based on splash risk; clean then disinfect per product directions (dwell time matters); handle laundry per plan [M07-4][M07-10].
- **Exposure response:** If needlestick, bite breaking skin, blood in eyes/mouth/open wound — **wash/flush immediately** (soap/water for skin, saline/water for mucous membranes — do not scrub/bleach wound), **report immediately** to supervisor/occupational health/infection prevention for risk assessment and possible post-exposure prophylaxis per CDC/OSHA agency protocol [M07-6][M07-6]. Timeliness matters (hours, not days).

7. Cleaning, Disinfection, Laundry, and Food — Setting-Specific, Product-Specific
Cleaning **removes** germs; disinfection **kills** remaining germs — both needed, in order, with correct product and **contact time** [M07-10][M07-4]:

- **High-touch surfaces:** Doorknobs, faucets, toys, shared bathroom fixtures — clean/disinfect per schedule and when visibly soiled or after illness; follow label dwell time (not “spray and wipe”) [M07-10].
- **Toys/childcare:** Mouthed toys cleaned after each use; cots/mats disinfected between children; diapering surface disinfected after each diaper per OCFS/DOH procedure [M07-8][M07-10].
- **Laundry:** Handle with gloves, bag without shaking/hugging to avoid dispersing particles, wash with detergent + appropriate temperature/dryer per policy; treat as potentially infectious if soiled with blood/body fluids [M07-10].
- **Food:** Separate food prep from diapering/toileting; hand hygiene before food handling; exclude ill food handlers per food-code/agency policy [M07-8].
- **What requires facilities services:** Bloodborne spill beyond small spot, outbreak deep clean, HVAC/negative-pressure, pest issues — escalate rather than improvising [M07-10].

8. Outbreaks and Exposures — What to Do, Whom to Notify, Confidentiality
Early notification limits spread [M07-2][M07-6][M07-9]:

- **Childcare/school:** Notify director/supervisor and families (de-identified — “a classroom exposure”) per agency + OCFS + local health department guidance; maintain attendance/illness log [M07-8][M07-9].
- **Senior/nursing/adult care/group home:** Notify supervisor + infection preventionist + supervisor’s supervisor / facility nurse manager per policy; facility may notify DOH (nursing homes 1-888-201-4563 [M07-2]/ adult care 1-866-893-6772) for reportable outbreaks — **do not confuse with abuse-reporting numbers — same phone but different purpose** (see §9 vs M05) [M07-3][M07-7a][M07-7b].
- **Occupational health for sharps/exposure:** Notify supervisor + occupational health immediately for risk assessment/testing/prophylaxis [M07-6].
- **Confidentiality:** Share only minimum necessary (date, setting, symptoms, attendance, actions taken) — do not name ill children/adults to unnecessary groups; follow FERPA/agency privacy where applicable [M07-9].

After-action: reflect with supervisor on hand-hygiene adherence, PPE availability, staffing adequacy, vaccine promotion barriers, and cleaning schedule [M07-10].

Glossary — Plain Language
**Airborne Precautions:** Small particles linger — requires facility engineering + fit-tested N95 — not cloth improvisation [M07-6].
**Contact Precautions:** Added gloves/gown/equipment steps for contact-spread illness on top of Standard Precautions [M07-4].
**Droplet Precautions:** Mask within close distance for droplet illness on top of Standard Precautions [M07-5].
**Dwell/Contact Time:** Label-required wet time for disinfectant to kill — not spray-wipe [M07-10].
**Exposure:** Needlestick, sharps injury, blood/body fluid splash to mucous membranes/broken skin — report immediately [M07-6].
**Exclusion:** Public-health decision to keep ill person from group per criteria — protection, not neglect [M07-8][M07-9].
**Hand Hygiene:** Soap/water or ≥60% sanitizer (with exceptions) before/after contact, toileting/diapering, food, glove removal [M07-4].
**Outbreak:** Two+ linked cases beyond expected — notify per chain [M07-2][M07-9].
**PPE:** Gloves, gowns, masks, eye protection — selected by splash/contact risk, changed between people [M07-4].
**Sharps Container 3/4 Rule:** Replace puncture-resistant container when ~3/4 full — prevent overfill injuries [M07-6].
**Standard Precautions:** Baseline for every person every time — any blood/body fluid/non-intact skin may carry infection [M07-5].
